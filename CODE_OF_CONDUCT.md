# IOIX Community Constitution

## The Constitution of the IOIX Community

Version 1.0

---

# Preamble

We, the contributors, maintainers, developers, users, and participants of IOIX,
recognizing that software is created through cooperation, responsibility,
knowledge sharing, and continuous improvement, establish this Constitution.

This Constitution defines the fundamental principles, rights, responsibilities,
limitations, and governance rules of the IOIX community.

IOIX is not only a collection of source code.

IOIX is a community built by people who create, review, improve, learn, and share.

The purpose of this Constitution is to protect:

- Freedom of contribution
- Technical excellence
- Respectful communication
- Open collaboration
- Responsible development

Every member of this community has value.

Every contribution has potential.

Every discussion can improve the project.

---

# Chapter I — Foundation

## Article 1 — Identity

### Section 1.1 — Definition

IOIX is an open-source software project focused on creating simple,
powerful, and extensible tools for project management, metadata handling,
automation, and developer workflows.

The project exists to provide practical solutions while maintaining:

- Simplicity
- Transparency
- Reliability
- Extensibility

---

### Section 1.2 — The Community

The IOIX community consists of all individuals participating in the project.

Members may include:

- Users
- Contributors
- Developers
- Maintainers
- Documentation authors
- Testers
- Reviewers
- Support members
- Idea contributors

Membership does not require:

- Professional experience
- Specific education
- A specific programming language background

Participation is based on contribution and respect.

---

## Article 2 — Purpose

### Section 2.1

The purpose of IOIX is to build useful software while creating a healthy
environment for technical collaboration.

The community aims to:

- Encourage innovation
- Share knowledge
- Improve software quality
- Support learning
- Respect different approaches

---

### Section 2.2

IOIX shall prefer:

Good design over unnecessary complexity.

Clear communication over confusion.

Long-term quality over temporary solutions.

Learning over gatekeeping.

---

# Chapter II — Fundamental Principles

## Article 3 — Principle of Respect

Respect is the foundation of the IOIX community.

Every member must treat others as human beings deserving dignity.

Respect includes:

- Listening to different opinions
- Accepting different experience levels
- Avoiding unnecessary hostility
- Communicating professionally

---

## Article 4 — Principle of Openness

The IOIX community values open discussion.

Members are encouraged to:

- Ask questions
- Share ideas
- Suggest improvements
- Discuss alternatives

No useful idea should be rejected only because it comes from a new member.

---

## Article 5 — Principle of Technical Freedom

Technical discussion is protected.

Members may disagree about:

- Architecture
- Code style
- Features
- Implementation
- Design decisions

A disagreement is not a conflict.

A disagreement becomes a conflict when respect disappears.

---

## Article 6 — Principle of Responsibility

Freedom requires responsibility.

Every member is responsible for:

- Their words
- Their actions
- Their contributions
- Their effect on the community

---

# Chapter III — Rights of Members

## Article 7 — Right to Participate

Every member has the right to participate in the community.

Participation includes:

- Creating discussions
- Reporting issues
- Suggesting ideas
- Improving documentation
- Contributing code
- Reviewing changes

---

## Article 8 — Right to Learn

Every person has the right to learn.

Beginner questions are allowed.

Learning mistakes are allowed.

A person must not be attacked because they are inexperienced.

---

## Article 9 — Right to Feedback

Every contributor has the right to receive useful feedback.

Feedback should:

- Explain problems
- Suggest improvements
- Focus on solutions

Feedback should not:

- Humiliate
- Attack
- Discourage

---

## Article 10 — Right to Be Heard

Community members may express opinions about the project.

Maintainers should consider reasonable feedback.

Not every suggestion must be accepted.

However, every serious suggestion deserves consideration.
# Chapter IV — Responsibilities of Members

## Article 11 — General Responsibility

Every member of the IOIX community has the responsibility to protect
the quality and health of the project.

Members shall act in a way that improves the community instead of damaging it.

Responsibility applies to:

- Code contributions
- Discussions
- Reports
- Reviews
- Public communication
- Community decisions

---

## Article 12 — Responsibility of Communication

Communication is one of the most important parts of an open-source project.

Members should communicate:

- Clearly
- Honestly
- Respectfully
- Constructively

Members should avoid:

- Ambiguous statements intended to create conflict
- Unnecessary arguments
- Repeated negative behavior
- Personal hostility

A strong opinion is allowed.

A harmful attitude is not.

---

## Article 13 — Responsibility of Contributors

A contributor who submits changes should attempt to ensure that their work is:

- Understandable
- Tested when possible
- Documented when necessary
- Compatible with project goals

A contributor should not intentionally submit:

- Broken implementations
- Malicious code
- Misleading information
- Unnecessary destructive changes

---

## Article 14 — Responsibility of Reviewers

Code reviewers have the responsibility to provide useful analysis.

A reviewer should evaluate:

- The technical quality
- The correctness
- The maintainability
- The compatibility

A reviewer should not evaluate:

- The personality of the contributor
- Their experience level
- Their personal background

---

## Article 15 — Responsibility of Maintainers

Maintainers have additional responsibilities because they hold project authority.

Maintainers should:

- Protect the project
- Apply rules fairly
- Explain important decisions
- Support contributors
- Preserve project values

Authority exists for service, not personal control.

---

# Chapter V — Community Protection

## Article 16 — Protection of Members

The IOIX community protects members from harmful behavior.

No member should experience:

- Harassment
- Threats
- Personal attacks
- Discrimination
- Unreasonable hostility

Protection applies equally to:

- New users
- Experienced developers
- Maintainers
- Contributors

---

## Article 17 — Equal Treatment

All participants should be treated according to their actions and contributions.

The community does not judge people based on:

- Experience level
- Location
- Education
- Background
- Personal characteristics

Technical discussion should remain technical.

---

# Chapter VI — Forbidden Behavior

## Article 18 — Personal Attacks

Personal attacks are prohibited.

Examples:

- Insulting someone instead of discussing their idea
- Attacking someone's intelligence
- Mocking someone for asking questions
- Attempting to embarrass another member

Criticism of work is allowed.

Attacking a person is not.

---

## Article 19 — Harassment

Harassment includes:

- Repeated unwanted communication
- Targeted hostility
- Intimidation
- Threatening behavior
- Attempts to make someone leave the community

Harassment is not acceptable in any form.

---

## Article 20 — Hate and Discrimination

The IOIX community does not accept hateful or discriminatory behavior.

This includes attacks based on:

- Identity
- Origin
- Personal characteristics
- Beliefs
- Background

Respect is required from everyone.

---

## Article 21 — Spam and Abuse

The following actions are prohibited:

- Repeated irrelevant messages
- Advertising unrelated content
- Automated abuse
- Flooding discussions
- Intentionally disrupting communication

---

## Article 22 — Malicious Actions

Members must not intentionally attempt to harm:

- The project
- The repository
- Other contributors
- Users

Examples include:

- Malicious code submission
- Deliberate vulnerabilities
- Destructive repository actions
- False reports intended to cause damage

---

# Chapter VII — Privacy and Information Protection

## Article 23 — Privacy Principle

The privacy of community members must be respected.

Members must not publish private information without permission.

Protected information includes:

- Private contact information
- Private conversations
- Personal documents
- Private accounts

---

## Article 24 — Responsible Disclosure

Security problems should be reported responsibly.

Members discovering vulnerabilities should:

- Report them privately when possible
- Provide useful details
- Avoid exploiting the problem

Security research is valuable.

Security abuse is not.

---

# Chapter VIII — Technical Discussion Rules

## Article 25 — Discussion of Ideas

Ideas may be challenged.

Designs may be criticized.

Code may be reviewed.

However, discussions must remain focused on the technical subject.

---

## Article 26 — Technical Debate

A healthy technical debate:

- Provides reasons
- Uses evidence
- Considers alternatives
- Searches for improvement

An unhealthy debate:

- Focuses on winning
- Attacks people
- Creates unnecessary conflict

---

## Article 27 — Different Opinions

The existence of disagreement does not mean someone is an enemy.

The community accepts that:

- Multiple solutions may exist
- Different approaches may have value
- Decisions require compromise
# Chapter IX — Governance and Authority

## Article 28 — Purpose of Governance

The governance system of IOIX exists to maintain stability,
quality, and fairness while preserving the open nature of the project.

Governance is not designed to create unnecessary control.

Governance exists to:

- Protect the project
- Organize decisions
- Resolve conflicts
- Maintain technical direction

---

# Chapter X — Maintainers

## Article 29 — Definition of Maintainer

A maintainer is a trusted member responsible for helping manage the project.

A maintainer may have responsibilities including:

- Reviewing contributions
- Managing releases
- Maintaining infrastructure
- Protecting project standards
- Guiding technical decisions

A maintainer is a servant of the project, not an owner of the community.

---

## Article 30 — Maintainer Authority

Maintainers may:

- Merge or reject contributions
- Close discussions
- Organize releases
- Modify project infrastructure
- Apply community rules

However, authority must always be used according to this Constitution.

---

## Article 31 — Limits of Authority

Maintainers must not use their position to:

- Silence reasonable criticism
- Remove people because of disagreement
- Give unfair advantages
- Ignore community rules

Authority without responsibility becomes abuse.

---

## Article 32 — Maintainer Decisions

Important decisions should consider:

- Technical quality
- Project goals
- Community feedback
- Long-term consequences

A maintainer may make a final decision when necessary.

However, explanation is encouraged.

---

## Article 33 — Temporary Emergency Authority

In urgent situations, maintainers may take immediate action to protect:

- Security
- Infrastructure
- Users
- Project integrity

Examples:

- Removing malicious content
- Blocking harmful activity
- Temporarily restricting access

Emergency actions should be reviewed afterward.

---

# Chapter XI — Decision Making

## Article 34 — Decision Principles

IOIX decisions should follow:

1. Evidence
2. Technical reasoning
3. Project goals
4. Community discussion

Popularity alone does not determine technical correctness.

---

## Article 35 — Technical Decisions

Technical decisions should consider:

- Simplicity
- Reliability
- Maintainability
- Performance
- Compatibility

A technically correct solution may require rejecting popular suggestions.

---

## Article 36 — Feature Proposals

Feature proposals should describe:

- The problem
- The proposed solution
- Possible alternatives
- Expected impact

A feature should exist because it provides value.

Not only because it is interesting.

---

## Article 37 — Breaking Changes

Breaking changes require additional consideration.

Before introducing a breaking change, contributors should consider:

- Existing users
- Migration difficulty
- Documentation requirements
- Long-term benefits

Breaking changes should have a clear reason.

---

# Chapter XII — Contributions

## Article 38 — Types of Contribution

Contribution is not limited to code.

Examples include:

- Programming
- Documentation
- Translation
- Testing
- Bug reporting
- Design suggestions
- Community support

All meaningful contributions have value.

---

## Article 39 — Contribution Review

Contributions may be reviewed for:

- Correctness
- Security
- Compatibility
- Style
- Project direction

Review is not rejection of the contributor.

Review exists to improve the project.

---

## Article 40 — Pull Requests

A pull request should:

- Explain the purpose
- Describe changes
- Include necessary context
- Avoid unrelated modifications

Large changes should be divided when possible.

---

## Article 41 — Rejection of Contributions

A contribution may be rejected because of:

- Technical problems
- Security concerns
- Conflict with project goals
- Maintenance difficulty

A rejection should focus on the contribution.

Not the contributor.

---

# Chapter XIII — Community Discussions

## Article 42 — Purpose of Discussions

Discussions exist for:

- Questions
- Ideas
- Planning
- Knowledge sharing
- Community interaction

---

## Article 43 — Discussion Quality

Participants should:

- Stay on topic
- Provide useful information
- Respect other opinions

Participants should avoid:

- Repeating arguments without purpose
- Creating unnecessary conflict
- Turning discussions into personal battles

---

## Article 44 — Closing Discussions

A discussion may be closed when:

- The question has been answered
- The topic is complete
- The discussion becomes harmful
- The discussion no longer provides value

Closing a discussion is a moderation action, not punishment by itself.
# Chapter XIV — Justice and Enforcement

## Article 45 — Purpose of Enforcement

Enforcement exists to protect the community, not to punish people unnecessarily.

The goal of enforcement is:

- Preventing harm
- Restoring healthy communication
- Protecting contributors
- Preserving project stability

Actions should always be reasonable and proportional.

---

# Chapter XV — Violations

## Article 46 — Definition of Violation

A violation occurs when a person intentionally or repeatedly breaks the principles of this Constitution.

A violation may involve:

- Communication
- Contributions
- Security
- Privacy
- Community behavior

---

## Article 47 — Severity Levels

Violations are classified into levels.

### Level I — Minor Violation

Examples:

- Unclear communication
- Small disruption
- Accidental mistakes
- Failure to follow a simple guideline

Possible actions:

- Friendly reminder
- Explanation
- Request for correction

---

### Level II — Serious Violation

Examples:

- Repeated disrespect
- Personal attacks
- Spam
- Ignoring community rules after warnings

Possible actions:

- Formal warning
- Temporary restriction
- Removal of specific content

---

### Level III — Critical Violation

Examples:

- Threats
- Malicious actions
- Security abuse
- Serious harassment
- Privacy violations

Possible actions:

- Immediate restriction
- Removal from community spaces
- Permanent ban

---

# Chapter XVI — Fair Enforcement

## Article 48 — Presumption of Good Intent

Members should generally be assumed to have good intentions unless evidence shows otherwise.

Mistakes are not automatically treated as malicious actions.

The community distinguishes between:

- Error
- Negligence
- Intentional harm

---

## Article 49 — Evidence and Review

Enforcement decisions should consider:

- Available information
- Context
- Previous behavior
- Impact of the action

Decisions should not be based only on assumptions.

---

## Article 50 — Right to Explanation

When reasonable, a person affected by moderation should receive:

- The reason for the action
- The relevant rule
- Possible next steps

---

# Chapter XVII — Appeals

## Article 51 — Right to Appeal

A person affected by a moderation action may request a review.

An appeal should include:

- The action being questioned
- The reason for disagreement
- Relevant information

---

## Article 52 — Appeal Review

Appeals should be reviewed by:

- The original maintainer with additional context
- Another maintainer
- Trusted community members when appropriate

---

## Article 53 — Appeal Outcomes

An appeal may result in:

- Keeping the decision
- Modifying the decision
- Removing the restriction
- Providing additional guidance

---

# Chapter XVIII — Conflict Resolution

## Article 54 — Peaceful Resolution

Conflicts should first attempt resolution through communication.

Participants should attempt to:

- Understand different viewpoints
- Explain concerns
- Find practical solutions

---

## Article 55 — Mediation

When direct discussion fails, maintainers may provide mediation.

Mediation aims to:

- Reduce conflict
- Clarify misunderstandings
- Find acceptable solutions

---

## Article 56 — Persistent Conflict

If a conflict repeatedly harms the community, maintainers may limit participation.

The purpose is protection of the community.

Not winning an argument.

---

# Chapter XIX — Security Law

## Article 57 — Security Responsibility

Security is a shared responsibility.

Every member should consider:

- User safety
- Data protection
- Code reliability

---

## Article 58 — Vulnerability Reporting

Security issues should be reported responsibly.

A responsible report should include:

- Description of the issue
- Steps to reproduce
- Possible impact
- Suggested mitigation if available

---

## Article 59 — Security Abuse

The following are prohibited:

- Exploiting vulnerabilities against others
- Publishing harmful methods without responsibility
- Intentionally weakening security
- Using project access for abuse

---

# Chapter XX — Privacy Law

## Article 60 — Personal Information

Personal information belongs to the individual.

Members must not share private information without permission.

---

## Article 61 — Private Communication

Private conversations must remain private unless:

- Permission is given

- Required by legitimate project administration
